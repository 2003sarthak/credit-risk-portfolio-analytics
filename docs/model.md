# Exploratory default model

The intended observation point is three months after origination. Repayment features use months 1–3 only. Bureau lines are restricted to those opened by that point. The line's status, outstanding amount and days past due have no report date, however, and customer attributes have no historical snapshot. They may reflect later information. The target has no default date, so a default may also precede the observation point. The label horizon is unspecified. Therefore the resulting scores and holdout metrics are exploratory and must not be represented as reliable prospective predictions or used for credit decisions. Date-filtering line openings alone does not solve this limitation.

Customer-grouped 80/20 splitting keeps all loans of the same customer in one partition. Median and most-frequent imputation, missing indicators for numeric fields, scaling and one-hot encoding are fit only on the training set. Compare a logistic regression baseline and random forest using held-out AUC, KS, Gini and AUC permutation importance. The displayed score is the logistic model for interpretability, not necessarily the best model. Per-account drivers are transformed-feature contributions to log odds relative to the model intercept, not causal effects. Scores for training accounts are in-sample and flagged; held-out metrics are the appropriate evaluation here.

Before production, obtain dated bureau/customer snapshots and dated default outcomes; build time-valid features, choose a forecast horizon, perform temporal and independent validation, assess calibration and fairness, and monitor drift, missingness, calibration and discrimination against matured outcomes. Use analyst review and appropriate model governance.
